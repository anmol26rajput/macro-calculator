kcal to eat / day

What is this number?

How much to eat each day for your goal. For fat loss it sits about 20% below what you burn, so your body covers the gap with stored fat. Close to it most days is what works; you don't need to be perfect.

Proteing

Builds and protects muscle, and keeps you full when you're eating less. Chicken, eggs, fish, paneer, Greek yogurt or curd, dal, tofu, whey. Spread it across your meals.

Carbsg

Your main fuel for training. Rice, roti, oats, potatoes, fruit. Carbs don't make you fat on their own; eating more total calories than you burn does. Eat more of them around your workouts.

Fatg

Keeps your hormones healthy and helps you absorb vitamins. Oil, ghee, butter, nuts, eggs, cheese. The easiest one to overdo: one tablespoon of oil is about 120 kcal, so measure it.

How to use: hit calories and protein every day, let carbs and fat flex, and only adjust by ~150 kcal if your weekly average weight hasn't moved in 2–3 weeks.

How is this worked out?

We replay your plan week by week: each week your weight changes, your calories burned drop (or rise) a little, and the next week is worked out from there. That's why progress slows near the end. Real life wobbles, so treat the date as a rough guide and recalculate every 4–5 kg.

kcal you eatvskcal you burn

The first number is your daily target, what you eat. The second is your maintenance (TDEE), what your body burns in a day. The gap between them is what changes your weight: eat less than you burn and your body uses stored fat to cover the difference. About 7,700 kcal of gap adds up to roughly 1 kg.

Maintenance (TDEE)What you burn in a day

Everything you burn in a day: your body at rest, moving around, and training. Eat this much and your weight stays about the same. It's an estimate, so treat it as a starting point and adjust based on your weekly average weight.

BMRBurned at total rest

What your body burns if you stayed in bed all day: breathing, heartbeat, keeping warm. You burn more than this every day, so don't diet below it.

Expected rateIf you hit it most days

How fast your weight should change. Your weight moves up and down daily with water, salt and food, so compare weekly averages, not single mornings. Week one often drops faster because of water.

Protein range1.6–2.2 g per kg

Research shows 1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg of body weight is enough to build and keep muscle. Your daily target sits inside this range. Landing anywhere in it is a good day, so you don't need to hit the exact number.

Protein per mealSplit over 4 meals

Spreading protein over a few meals helps your body use it for muscle better than one huge meal. Think one main protein food per meal: chicken, fish, eggs, paneer, tofu or a whey shake.

Fibre14 g per 1,000 kcal

Keeps digestion regular and keeps you full, which helps a lot on a diet. Vegetables, fruit, oats, dal, beans, whole grains. Most people eat about half of this.

Water~35 ml per kg

A rough daily baseline. Drink more in hot weather and on gym days. If your pee is pale yellow, you're drinking enough.

Weekly caloriesYour daily target × 7

For fat loss the week matters more than any single day. Eating a bit less on weekdays to leave room for a weekend meal out is fine, as long as the weekly total stays the same.

What does BMI mean?

Your weight (kg) divided by your height (m) squared. It's a quick screen for health risk, not a measure of body fat, so muscular people often show "overweight" while being lean. South Asians tend to carry more fat at the same BMI, so risk starts lower (23 instead of 25). Switch to South Asian cut-offs if that's you.

Why do the numbers change?

Protein stays high so you keep or build muscle, fat stays about the same, and carbs take whatever calories are left. That's why carbs change the most between goals.

How it's calculated
  • BMR: Mifflin-St Jeor, the most accurate everyday formula.
  • Activity: desk job + lifting 3–4x is honestly "lightly active" (×1.375). Many calculators bump gym-goers to ×1.55, which overestimates and stalls fat loss.
  • Fat loss: 20% under maintenance, never below your BMR or 1,500 / 1,200 kcal (men / women). Lean gain: 10% over. Bulk: 15% over.
  • Goal weight: if it's below your current weight, protein and fat are based on your goal weight, since that's the body you're building toward. The timeline replays your plan week by week as your weight changes.
  • Protein: 2.0 g/kg when cutting, 1.8 g/kg otherwise. Fat: 0.8 g/kg, at least 20% of calories. Carbs: the rest. Above a BMI of 30, grams are based on a BMI-30 weight so targets stay realistic.
  • Expected rate: ~7,700 kcal ≈ 1 kg of body fat. Aim for 0.5–1% of bodyweight a week on a cut. Week-one drops are mostly water.
  • BMI uses WHO cut-offs (25 / 30). For South Asians, risk starts lower (23 / 27.5). Lifters with muscle can read "overweight" while lean.
  • Fibre: 14 g per 1,000 kcal (US dietary guidelines). Water: ~35 ml per kg is a rough baseline, add more for heat and training.
  • These are starting estimates, not medical advice. Your scale trend is the real test.
